🗄️ Data Model · thin, account‑led CRM over the insurance engines

Operationalizes "thin CRM over systems of record" — CRM orchestrates demand & relationships; SAP/MCS keep policy, claims and quoting.

DM‑01

Account is the centre of gravity

Custom buildDesign pattern

One Account entity with an accountType discriminator (Household · Business · Broker) covers all party types.

Avoids Salesforce's Person‑Account one‑way door and B2C/B2B split. Mobi24 and broker reuse the same account model, just extended.

vs SFFSC pulls you into heavyweight Policy/Claim/Producer objects → CRM becomes a 2nd policy system. vs BSIBSI ships Swiss‑insurance data models too — we match the fit and stay schema‑flexible on open Dataverse, not a packaged suite schema.
DM‑02

Portfolio lives at the account, not the contact

Design pattern

Policy and Claim hang off Account. Contact connects via ContactRole (Hauptkontakt / Mitentscheider / Kontext / MaklerBetreuer) — never owning the portfolio.

Swiss household has multiple policies & stakeholders; contact = relationship role, not portfolio owner.

DM‑03

Thin CRM over systems of record

Design pattern

Policy, Claim, Quote carry external reference keys to SAP / MCS / quoting engines. CRM does not become a second policy system.

RFP §4.1.3 explicit requirement — Versicherungstechnik stays in existing apps; avoids the FSC over‑modelling trap.

vs SF & BSIBoth incumbents tempt you to absorb insurance logic into the CRM suite. Our bet is the one the RFP asked for — thin orchestration over the engines — so Mobiliar keeps one source of truth and avoids dual‑system reconciliation.
DM‑04

Demand is a closed loop

Design pattern

Lead → LeadCluster → Opportunity → Quote, with NextBestAction + Activity (call/transcript/AI summary) feeding a learning signal back.

LeadCluster = anti‑over‑contact bundling so one household isn't contacted 5×.

DM‑05

Claim/Service‑to‑lead is first‑class

Design pattern

Case and Claim can each generate a qualified Lead with full context (e.g. Schaden SCH‑77310 → Lead → Sales Case VF‑2026‑0412).

DM‑06

Consent modelled per contact, per channel

Custom build

Consent = {Phone · Email · SMS · Newsletter} × {Allowed · Denied · NotRelevant} + source + capturedOn. Surfaced as a guardrail across the whole UI.

Drives the outbound opt‑in checks (see OB‑03) and the voice consent gate.

DM‑07

Custom cr7e8_ entities, single physical lookup columns

Custom buildGotcha / lesson

Custom cr7e8_offer, cr7e8_policy, cr7e8_campaign. Define all lookups in one create_table call — create/delete/recreate produced duplicate physical columns (broker corruption).

DM‑08

Lead scoring: use score columns directly

Gotcha / lesson

Predictive‑scoring nav property msdyn_predictivescoreid is not provisioned → $expand returns 400. Use msdyn_leadscore / msdyn_leadgrade / msdyn_leadscoretrend on the lead.

DM‑09

@odata.bind casing must match schema name

Gotcha / lesson

Navigation property names are case‑sensitive. cr7e8_Lead@odata.bind (capital L) silently rejected the whole createRecord → use lowercase cr7e8_lead@odata.bind.

👨‍👩‍👧 B2C / Private‑customer modeling · 2.3M Privatkunden · 2M Interessenten · 300k Prospects

Mobiliar is overwhelmingly retail. The same account‑led model carries B2C — the household is the account, the private person is a contact‑with‑roles, and identity is shared with PDV.

B2C‑01

Household‑as‑Account (not Person‑Account)

Design pattern

Account is a party container, not literally a household — accountType = Household · Business · Broker. For B2C the container is the household: a private customer is a Contact (natural person) under a Household Account; policies & claims sit at household level (Gebäude/Hausrat/Haftpflicht are inherently shared). A single‑person household is still an account — one consistent shape.

Grounded in PDV, not invented: the household maps to the partner master's existing grouping — Account(Household) ↔ PDV Personengruppe, Account(Business) ↔ Organisation, Contact ↔ natürliche Person. CRM mirrors PDV's authoritative grouping read‑only (thin‑CRM), rather than defining its own.

vs SFSalesforce Person Accounts are a one‑way door and clash when household + business + broker must coexist in one org. vs BSIWe match BSI's Swiss household/Partnerverbund concept on an open schema, aligned to PDV Personengruppen.
B2C‑02

Insurance roles via ContactRole (VN / VP / Zahler)

Design pattern

One person plays many roles across household policies — Versicherungsnehmer, versicherte Person, Mitversicherte, Zahler, Hauptkontakt — modelled in ContactRole, not duplicated as separate customers.

Reflects Swiss reality: the policyholder, the insured and the payer are often different people in one household.

B2C‑03

Prospect (CRM‑owned) vs Interessent/Kunde (PDV‑mastered)

Design patternLesson

Prospects (~300k) are CRM‑owned — they don't exist in PDV yet. Interessenten & Kunden are mastered in PDV and replicated read‑only into CRM. Qualifying a prospect promotes it into the PDV person master.

Keeps the partner master authoritative (RFP §5.1) while CRM owns the top‑of‑funnel that PDV deliberately doesn't hold.

B2C‑04

B2C scale → thin contacts, reach via CI not seats

Design pattern

2.3M customers + 2M Interessenten + ~5M partners + millions of campaign interactions/yr. Contact records stay thin references; mass engagement runs through Customer Insights (outbound reach), not per‑seat CRM licences.

vs SFAvoids Data Cloud + marketing‑cloud sprawl at retail volume. vs BSIHyperscale Dataverse + Fabric handle the volume and analytics a point‑suite strains on.
B2C‑05

Consent & preference center at retail scale

Custom build

Per‑contact, per‑channel consent (DM‑06) is the mandatory gate for every B2C outbound — mass opt‑in/opt‑out, preference center, revFADP. Surfaced in the 360 and enforced in flows (optin / donotphone).

B2C mass personal‑data processing makes this the highest‑risk compliance surface — it's a first‑class entity, not a checkbox.

B2C‑06

Self‑service portal & app as B2C contact points

Design pattern

The Privatkunden‑Portal + mobile app are digital contact points in the hybrid customer journey; their status/events write back into CRM (e.g. the customer‑app status screen) so advisors see the same journey the customer sees.

Versicherungstechnik stays in the portal (eigenentwicklung); CRM orchestrates the journey & context around it.

🎯 Lead modeling · a Lead = an expression of interest on a pre‑existing person

Mobiliar's semantics: a person (existing Contact or Prospect) can hold many leads, and leads can be grouped. That inverts the native D365 "lead‑as‑person‑stub" model — so we keep the native table but change how it's wired.

✔ Decision — chosen model

Native lead table, inverted: Lead = interest event linked to an existing person

The person always pre‑exists as a Contact (a Prospect is just a Contact with lifecycleStage = Prospect; an existing client is Interessent/Kunde). A Lead references that Contact via parentcontactid (and the household via parentaccountid) instead of embedding a person stub. Many leads per person; a custom LeadCluster bundles them.

OPTION A — rejected

Native lead as the person stub

Anti‑pattern here

Use the lead's embedded fields (subject, contactname, companyname) and qualify‑creates‑contact.

Why not: a person can't hold multiple leads without duplicating the person stub; no clean grouping; qualify would create duplicate Contacts for people who already exist in PDV/CRM.

OPTION B — rejected

Fully custom Lead entity

High cost

Build cr7e8_lead from scratch, ignore the native table.

Why not: loses native predictive lead scoring (msdyn_leadscore/grade/trend), BPF, routing/assignment, the "Meine Leads" stream views and NBA — all of which we'd have to rebuild.

OPTION C — chosen

Native lead, inverted + LeadCluster

Native+ custom group

Keep the native lead for scoring/BPF/routing/cockpit; always set parentcontactid; add LeadCluster for grouping.

Why: best of both — free AI scoring & tooling, plus Mobiliar's "person owns many leads" semantics and anti‑over‑contact bundling.

LD‑01

Prospect = Contact lifecycle stage, not a separate type

Design pattern

Prospect / Interessent / Kunde is a lifecycle stage on one Contact. The lead→person link is uniform regardless of stage; promotion is a status change (+ PDV sync), not a record migration.

Lets a prospect accumulate multiple leads before ever becoming a client — impossible if the prospect is a lead.

LD‑02

Grouping: LeadCluster per person, rolled up to household

Custom build

LeadCluster bundles a person's open leads (and can roll up to the household Account) so the advisor runs one conversation, not five — the anti‑over‑contact guardrail.

Swiss household holds many policies → many parallel interests; grouping prevents contact fatigue.

LD‑03

Qualify without creating duplicate people

Lesson

Because the Contact (and household) already exist, lead qualification is configured to convert to Opportunity only — reuse the existing Contact/Account, never spawn a new one.

LD‑04

Source typing: claim/service/campaign‑to‑lead

Design pattern

A typed leadSource keeps the closed loop legible — Schaden‑to‑Lead, Service‑to‑Lead, Campaign, Inbound — feeding scoring, NBA and attribution back to the originating Case/Claim/Campaign.

vs SF & BSIThe same person‑centric, multi‑lead, grouped model holds across sales, after‑sales and broker channels on one schema — no separate lead silos per cloud/module.

🤖 AI & Agents · event/scheduled agents · NBA at scale · human‑in‑the‑loop UI

What is native first‑party Copilot/CI vs what must be framed as an extension — and the "human → agent" interaction pattern.

★ Key pattern — agents at scale, humans decide

Event‑triggered & scheduled agents generate recommendations; the custom UI is where humans act

Autonomous agents run on events (case/claim created, policy renewal, lifecycle change) or on a schedule (nightly batch), scoring the whole book and producing Next‑Best‑Actions at scale. They don't act on the customer directly — the recommendation lands in the advisor's cockpit as an explainable action card the human accepts, edits or dismisses, and that decision feeds the learning loop.

vs SF & BSISame trigger taxonomy as governed outbound (Automated · Instant · Scheduled). Agents are grounded on one Dataverse and stay advisory — keeping a human accountable for every customer‑facing act, which a regulated Swiss insurer needs.

AI‑01

"Template → human → agent" interactive realism

Design pattern

4‑stage state machine: scaffold → human entry → Teams invite → WorkIQ agent reads transcript & writes back attendance, decisions, extracted risk tasks.

Every agent‑touched row flashes + carries a purple "Aus Transkript" badge so the agent's contribution is unmistakable. Records start empty and fill through actions (not pre‑populated).

vs SF & BSIThe agent grounds on Teams/M365 content natively (transcript, calendar, Word). SF reaches M365 through connectors; BSI is not an M365‑native fabric — neither makes "agent works where the advisor already works" structural.
AI‑02

Consistent Copilot card grammar

Design pattern

Every Copilot card = urgency coding + a specific CTA. Next‑Best‑Action surfaced along all tasks, leads and conversations.

AI‑03

Native CI‑Journeys AI — present as‑is

Native

Credible & native: trigger/segment journeys, A/B with control group, run‑time AI channel optimization, NL query‑assist segments, gen‑AI content ideas/rewrite, multi‑language dynamic content, lead scoring + NBA to sales.

vs SFCopilot grounds on the same Dataverse — no Data Cloud prerequisite, no per‑conversation premium. vs BSIWe bring hyperscale Azure OpenAI + agentic depth & cadence a Swiss point‑suite can't match — while keeping CH residency.
AI‑04

Paid / Lookalike / Budget — frame as extensions

Extension

Paid Meta/Google = not a native send channel → audience activation via export connectors. Lookalike = not native CI‑Data → Azure ML. Budget/ROI/CPL = custom campaign table + Power BI.

Pages deliberately isolate spend/ROI on the analytical cockpit, away from CI‑Journeys.

AI‑05

Real‑time voice transcript = native voice path only

NativePlatform constraint

Live transcript + Copilot voice (summaries, NBA, live "Buddy") only on the native ACS voice channel; the ACS TranscriptionData WebSocket enables custom AOAI extraction. Not available while Luware owns the call.

See the separate Voice Integration Architecture page.

vs SFService Cloud Voice needs Amazon Connect / BYO telephony — another vendor & contract. vs BSINo hyperscale voice/AI telephony stack. Native ACS voice = fewer seams for a 24/7 assistance operation.
AI‑06

Autonomous Case Management Agent for prefill

Native

OOB Case Management Agent predicts & populates case fields (incl. custom) at conversation accept/end — chosen over a custom AOAI build for case prefill (lowest effort, no custom model).

AI‑07

Agents recommend at scale; the human acts in the UI

Design patternNative

Event/scheduled agents score the book and emit NBAs at scale; advisors accept/edit/dismiss them in the cockpit. Agents are advisory by design, not auto‑acting on the customer.

Keeps a human accountable for every customer‑facing decision (regulatory fit) and turns each decision into a learning signal.

📤 Outbound Messaging · SMS / WhatsApp / voice — governed, consent‑checked

Outbound digital is template‑, config‑ and flow‑driven by design; only voice cold‑start is ad‑hoc.

OB‑01

No native agent cold‑start for SMS/WhatsApp

Platform constraint

No "compose new SMS/WhatsApp" button. Every outbound digital message routes through message template → Outbound Configuration → Power Automate flow calling msdyn_InvokeOutboundAPI.

Deliberate D365 architecture favouring governed, auditable, consent‑checked outbound. Triggers: Automated · Instant · Scheduled.

OB‑02

WhatsApp 24‑hour / template rule

Platform constraint

Outside the 24‑hour customer‑service window only pre‑approved templates may be sent. This is Meta's WhatsApp Business rule — universal across all vendors, not a D365 limit.

OB‑03

SMS gated by consent + A2P registration

Platform constraint

Opt‑in (optin) + donotphone enforced at the API level; 10DLC/A2P + carrier rules. Technically cold‑startable → gating is compliance + product design. Provider = Twilio or ACS.

OB‑04

Reply‑becomes‑inbound model

Native

Outbound template/flow → customer replies → reply enters as a normal inbound omnichannel conversation, routed and assigned to a rep.

OB‑05

Custom "Send SMS" button is feasible & honest

Custom build

A web‑resource screen can invoke msdyn_InvokeOutboundAPI via Xrm.WebApi.online.execute (or trigger a flow). The button is just the invocation surface; template + config + optin still apply.

OB‑06

Voice cold‑start stays native

Native

Unlike digital, outbound voice is ad‑hoc from the dialpad / click‑to‑call — used in the softphone screen.

🎨 UI / UX & Platform · operational workspaces, not static reports

Make screens feel like live model‑driven forms; respect Omnichannel platform constraints.

UI‑01

Operational workspace, not a static report

Design pattern

Buttons must demonstrate real outcomes — status flips, a new row appears, progress ring advances. Records start empty/sparse and fill through actions.

Top UX fix: mockups read "plasticky" when they show a finished state with no way it got there.

UI‑02

Visual process + hero narrative

Design pattern

Funnel charts on list pages, progress rings on detail pages, timeline views on queues. Hero banner = KPI summary + narrative punchline ("CHF 42k opportunity in 37 cases").

UI‑03

Compliance gates rendered in the UI

Design pattern

Don't sell during an open complaint (salesBlock); portfolio stays at account level; consent visible before contact. Guardrails are visible, not just back‑end.

UI‑04

In‑app navigation, no new tab

Custom buildDesign pattern

mobiNav(etn,id,url) → Xrm.Navigation.openForm (same tab), falls back to top‑window redirect. No target=_blank / window.open.

UI‑05

Mockup → implemented screen pattern

Design pattern

Strip chrome/nav, add Xrm binding, wire live data via Web API. Versioned redesigns kept side‑by‑side (‑v2‑) for iteration.

vs SFThese Fluent cockpits neutralize SF's "better UX than vanilla D365" claim — and GitHub Copilot turns the mockups into PCF/model‑driven screens at near‑config speed. vs BSINo Lightning/BSI component ceiling on the differentiated cockpits; full control of the experience layer.
UI‑06

Omnichannel stream views can't be customized

Gotcha / lessonPlatform constraint

OCStreamControl only resolves the 3 OOB system stream views (myitems/openitems/closeditems). Custom savedquery → empty fetchXml= → 400. Filter upstream via routing/queues, not by editing views.

UI‑07

Terminology & branding conventions

Design pattern

B2B institutional‑account language (not B2C/contact); generic/neutral product branding; account‑level portfolio context in contact views.

UI‑08

Consistent shell & cross‑links

Design pattern

Shared nav bar (logo, search, sections, active‑state) across all pages; verified cross‑links (cockpit → Brunner 360 → claim/lead records). Harmonized verbs + confirmation toasts.

UI‑09

Fluent design system + standardized page anatomy

Design pattern

Every screen = same anatomy: chrome bar · left nav · breadcrumb · KPI bar · command bar · tab strip · status bar. Fluent tokens (Segoe UI, brand #0078d4, Material Symbols), cards radius 8.

Recognition over recall — users don't relearn each screen; one token set keeps DE/FR/IT + brand theming consistent.

UI‑10

Copilot is a tab, not a right sidebar

Design pattern

Copilot embedded as a content tab with exactly four structured action cards: Urgent · Risk · Opportunity · Insight — each with type label, metric, context and a 1‑click CTA.

Keeps AI inside the work surface and the main content full‑width; insights are acts, not passive text.

UI‑11

Systems of record render read‑only & visually distinct

Design pattern

External‑system data (policy/claim/quote from SAP/MCS) shown with muted colours, lock icons, no edit buttons, a sync‑timestamp banner.

The thin‑CRM principle (DM‑03) made visible — the UI itself signals "CRM orchestrates, doesn't own this."

vs SF & BSIWhere FSC/BSI invite in‑CRM editing of insurance objects, our UI deliberately marks them as references — one source of truth, no reconciliation.
UI‑12

Accessibility: never colour‑alone, F/Z scan

Design pattern

Urgency is coded and labelled (icon + text, not colour only — ~8% of men are colour‑blind). Most important metric top‑left along the F/Z path; related content grouped (Gestalt proximity).

UI‑13

Data‑viz fitness + deltas/baselines

Design pattern

Prefer length & 2D position (bars, funnels) which read preattentively; avoid pie/donut/gauge/3D for quantities. Every number carries a delta & baseline so it means something.

Operational screens optimise speed‑to‑status; analytical cockpits optimise trend & comparison.

UI‑14

DE / FR / IT localization is first‑class

Design patternEdge

Labels read naturally to a Swiss insurance professional (Anliegenfall, Schadenfall, Offerte, Makler); multilingual data model and consistent token set keep three languages clean.

vs SFTrilingual bespoke UX is a real translation/maintenance tax on Lightning. vs BSIBSI is natively Swiss‑multilingual — we match it, and add M365/Copilot multilingual generation on top.
UI‑15

UX governed by a grounded scoring framework

Design pattern

Screens scored on 11+ dimensions (NN/g · Baymard · Pencil&Paper · Fluent): JTBD clarity, role relevance, one‑click reachability, AI actionability, process clarity, viz fitness, accessibility, demo readiness. Fixes prioritised by lowest score × highest impact.

Design quality is a measured, repeatable gate — not taste.

⚔️ Competitive narrative · vs Salesforce (FSC) & BSI (Customer Suite)

The advantage is operating‑model fit, not feature count. We meet each rival on their home turf and win on the architecture the RFP actually asks for.

CMP‑SF

vs Salesforce — they'll benchmark against vanilla D365

Edge

Their strawman: "better UX, mature AI/Agentforce, prebuilt FSC insurance model, Person‑Account householding, one platform."

How we collapse it: the Fluent cockpits + account‑led model mean Mobiliar isn't buying vanilla D365. Thin‑CRM beats the FSC "rebuild insurance in CRM" trap; Household‑as‑Account beats the Person‑Account one‑way door (household + business + broker in one model); the person‑centric, multi‑lead, grouped lead model has no per‑cloud silos; Copilot grounds on Dataverse with no Data Cloud tax; native ACS voice vs Amazon Connect; mixed multi‑persona licensing for 6,500 staff vs full‑seat clouds; GitHub Copilot + mockups‑as‑accelerator removes the "custom UX needs developers" objection.

On Agentforce: our agents are event/scheduled, advisory, human‑accountable and grounded on one Dataverse — stronger governance & economics for a regulated insurer than per‑conversation autonomous agents.

Only claim left alive: AppExchange / named‑logo breadth — reframe to fit + M365 + Swiss residency.

So what — in business terms
  • One view of the whole relationship. A family, the company they own, and their broker all live in a single customer view — advisors stop stitching together separate B2C and B2B systems and see every policy and opportunity in one place.
  • No second policy system to maintain. The CRM sits on top of your insurance engines instead of copying them — less to build, less to keep in sync, lower long‑term IT cost.
  • No hidden AI "data tax". Copilot works directly on your data with no extra data platform to license and run — the AI bill stays predictable.
  • Pay only for what each person needs. 6,500 staff get mixed, role‑based licences instead of a full premium seat for everyone — a materially smaller licence bill.
  • Live in weeks, not quarters. These exact screens become the working product, so you see value fast with far less custom development.

Bottom line: with Salesforce you rebuild your insurance business inside the CRM and pay for a data platform and full seats. With us: lower total cost, faster to go live, and one trusted view of every customer.

CMP‑BSI

vs BSI — the comfortable Swiss incumbent

Edge

Their strength: Swiss‑made, deep CH insurance/banking references, local data residency, packaged insurance journeys — "low‑risk, knows our market."

How we neutralize the comfort play: we match Swiss residency (Azure Zürich/Geneva, FINMA‑aligned), the household/Partnerverbund model and multilingual DE/FR/IT fit — then out‑scale on what a point‑suite can't: M365‑native fabric (Teams/Outlook/Word), hyperscale Azure OpenAI + event/scheduled agents emitting NBAs across 2.3M customers, one Dataverse + Fabric/Power BI analytics, a global ISV/partner ecosystem, and continuous innovation investment. BSI's data model & extensibility are tied to BSI's own stack; ours is an open schema.

Positioning: "BSI's Swiss comfort, without the platform ceiling."

So what — in business terms
  • You give up nothing on safety. Swiss data residency, your household/Partnerverbund model and full DE/FR/IT support are all there — the same low‑risk comfort BSI sells you.
  • No growth ceiling. A point‑suite caps what you can extend; this is an open platform that keeps scaling as your needs grow, so you're not re‑buying in three years.
  • AI that works across all 2.3M customers. Next‑best‑action recommendations run at full scale, not just on a few packaged journeys — more relevant offers, more advisor productivity.
  • Lives where your staff already work. Inside Teams, Outlook and Word — less change, faster adoption, no extra app to learn.

Bottom line: the same Swiss comfort and low risk as BSI — but on a platform that grows with you and applies AI across every customer, instead of one that caps out as you scale.
